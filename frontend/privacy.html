Privacy Policy

Banpani is built so that people can be helped without being exposed. This page states exactly what is collected, why, and what never leaves the system.

1. The short version

No accounts, no logins, no names required. The public map shows needs and places, never people. Phone numbers are optional, never shown publicly, and never included in any download or interface response. Money is never collected or displayed. Everything here can be checked against the open source code.

2. What we collect

  • Reports: a location (a pin on the map), what is needed, roughly how many people are affected, and any details you type.
  • An optional phone number, only if you choose to give one, so that a relief volunteer can call back. The field is clearly marked optional.
  • Photographs you choose to upload as evidence of the situation or of relief work. The location information (GPS data) is removed from every photo, in your browser, before it is uploaded.
  • Offers, blocked roads, facility status, hazards, escape routes, and similar map markers that you add to help coordinate.
  • Convoy, drop-off, and organisation listings submitted by relief groups, including the group name they enter, which is public by design.
  • Verification votes (confirm, delivered, not real, flag), limited to one per device per item.
  • A volunteer email address, only if you sign up on the volunteer page. It is stored encrypted and used only to contact you if a disaster is confirmed near you. See retention and your rights below.

3. Why we collect it

For one purpose only: coordinating disaster relief and recovery, so that affected areas are found, effort is not duplicated, and reports can be verified by the community. We do not use this data for anything else. We do not sell it, we do not share it with advertisers, and we do not build a profile of anyone.

4. What is public and what is not

Public: the location and content of reports, hazard areas, coverage, drop-off points, organisation listings, photographs, and an activity log where each participant appears only as an anonymous identifier.

Never public: phone numbers, names (we do not ask for them on reports), volunteer email addresses, and network (IP) addresses. Phone numbers are excluded from all bulk data, downloads, and interface responses. A number can be revealed only one at a time, through a recorded request, when a volunteer actually needs to coordinate about that report, and every such access is logged.

5. Consent

Everything you submit, you submit deliberately. There is no account to link things to, and the report data you enter is never used to profile you. Giving a phone number is a choice, made at the moment of reporting, for the stated purpose of being contacted about that need. Signing up as a volunteer is a separate, deliberate choice, and you can withdraw at any time.

6. Analytics and cookies

To understand how many people the tool is reaching, and on which kinds of devices, Banpani uses Google Analytics. Like standard web analytics, it may set cookies and shares your network address with Google. It is used only for aggregate visitor counts, never to identify you, and it is never linked to any report, photo, phone number, or volunteer email. It is entirely optional to you: browser "Do Not Track" settings and ordinary analytics or ad blockers stop it, and Banpani works fully without it. A cookieless, first-party counter also runs on our own server, storing only a daily tally, so we can measure reach even when analytics is blocked.

7. Open data

Because Banpani only works if everyone can see the same picture, the non-personal data (needs, hazard extent, coverage, listings, photographs) is open, and dedicated to the public domain under Creative Commons Zero (CC0 1.0). It can be read by anyone through the public interface and the periodic situation report, and reused for any purpose. Personal identifiers are stripped from all of it by design.

8. Money never touches Banpani

Banpani never asks for, stores, or displays a bank account number, a payment identifier, a card, or any other means of collecting money, neither for the project nor for anyone listed on it. No donation or payment ever flows through this system. This is deliberate. It removes the single most common route for fraud during a disaster, and it means there is no financial data to protect in the first place.

9. How long we keep it

Reports and coverage data are kept for the duration of the disaster response and its recovery phase, because a record of who was helped, and where, matters for accountability. This record then moves into a public archive.

Phone numbers are deleted once the response has wound down, and in any case within three months of submission. Volunteer email addresses are kept until you ask to be removed, or until you tell us you no longer wish to be contacted.

10. Your rights

If you want a report, a photograph, a phone number, or your volunteer sign-up removed, whether it is yours or one concerning you, use the Contact form on the map, or email the address in the Contact section below. A maintainer will act on it. You do not need to prove anything elaborate. If a reasonable person would want it gone, it goes.

11. Where the data lives

The database runs on a single server operated by the maintainer, and the static site is served through a content delivery network. The full source code is open, so every claim on this page can be checked against what the system actually does: github.com/parthajy/banpani.

12. Applicable law

Banpani is operated from India and handles personal data in line with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, and the Information Technology Act, 2000. The same privacy-by-design principles, namely data minimisation and purpose limitation, align with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), so that redeployments of this open-source code elsewhere start from a compliant baseline.

13. Children

Banpani is a general-purpose relief tool and is not directed at children. It does not knowingly collect personal information from children. Because there are no accounts and no profiles, and because the only optional personal detail is a phone number or a volunteer email given by the person submitting it, the service collects no more from a child than from anyone else. If you believe a child's personal information has been submitted and should be removed, contact us and we will remove it.

14. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the change will be visible in the public commit history of the repository, in the open, like everything else here. The date at the top of this page reflects the most recent update.

15. Contact

For any privacy question or request, email the volunteer behind Banpani at parthajy@gmail.com, or use the Contact button on the map. No phone numbers are shown to anyone.
